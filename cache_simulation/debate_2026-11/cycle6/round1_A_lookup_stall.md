# cycle 6 round 1, agent A: cutting the exposed lookup stall at 103 GB

read: FINAL_best_build.md, cycle3 round8 (96-thread profile), cycle5 round6 (K2_B sweep), cycle2 round3_C, cycle4 round1_A_fixed_costs, fcfg.csv, pf.csv. luna (read-only cat/grep): compact_hash.cc, classify.cc pipeline loop, Makefile, /sys THP files, /proc/vmstat, /proc/meminfo, /proc/buddyinfo, numa_balancing. nothing run. tags: [L] read on luna, [M] measured earlier by us, [A] my arithmetic, [P] published, [G] guess.

## 0. what the numbers say before any idea

classification time at 103 GB: final T=128 no pre-pass is 6.69 s wall, minus about 3.2 s load and exit, leaves about 3.3 s [M, A]. the same arithmetic at 8 GB gives 1.61 - 0.3 = about 1.3 s. the 8 GB table is also far bigger than L3, so DRAM latency is the same in both. the excess of about 2 s (30% of the 6.7 s run) is the most any "bigger table" cost can be worth at 103 GB. it still contains some teardown (munmap of 52k huge pages, cycle4 X2, 0.25 to 0.5 s) that is not a lookup stall. at 16 GB the excess is only about 0.2 s. so every idea below is a 103 GB idea; at 8/16 GB each is worth 0 to 1%.

is it bandwidth or latency bound? [A] lookups per run about 218M (upper bound; dedup removes some), classification about 3.3 s, so 66M lookups/s, each one 64 B line: 4.2 GB/s. DRAM peak on 2 sockets x 8 channels DDR5-4800 is about 2 x 307 = 614 GB/s [P, G], achievable random maybe half. the table uses about 1.4% of peak. not bandwidth bound.
in-flight lines by Little's law: 66M/s x 130 ns (local DRAM, G; remote is 1.5 to 2x) = about 9 lines in flight machine-wide, against 96 cores x 16 line fill buffers = 1536 [P: Golden Cove has 16 L1D fill buffers, up from 12, AnandTech]. on average 0.6% of the memory-level parallelism the machine owns is used. per thread: 218M / 128 threads / 3.3 s = one lookup per 1.9 us, so even a fully exposed 130 ns miss would be 7% of a lookup's time. yet K2_B 4 -> 64 gained 17% at T=96 (7.87 -> 6.88 s, kb.csv). so the real penalty of an un-hidden lookup is 2 to 3x the nominal DRAM latency: something besides the DRAM round trip is attached to each miss (page walk, remote-socket access, hint-fault and migration traffic from NUMA balancing). key reading: the machine is neither bandwidth bound nor MLP-capacity bound; it pays a per-lookup extra that deeper batches no longer hide (flat 64 to 128, worse at 256 in pf.csv).
caveat: the 29.5% GetAt share is a share of samples over the whole process, which includes the 3.2 s load when most threads spin, plus perf skid. user time rises 255 s -> 324 s from T=96 to T=128 at identical wall [M], so it is mostly spin and cannot measure stalls. use wall-time deltas, not shares.

## 2. ranked ideas

### I1. pipeline lookups across reads (a batch may span reads) [mechanism found in the loop]
mechanism: when a read has fewer than B lookups, fill() returns n < la_batch, exhausted=true, and the resolve loop runs right after the prefetches were issued: zero lookahead, the whole miss is exposed. this also explains the plateau: at 116 lookups per read a batch of 128 or 256 turns a read into one unoverlapped batch. fix: software-pipeline at read granularity: scan, hash and prefetch the next read (or next 2) before resolving the current one, with a ring of 2 reads' PfSlot arrays. per-read state and output order are unchanged.
gain at 103 GB [A, G]: depends on the read-length histogram. if 30% of lookups sit in reads shorter than B and each exposes about 300 ns (section 0): 218M x 0.3 x 300 ns / 128 = 0.15 s (2%); if all reads are short, 0.5 s (7%). 8/16 GB: 0.02 to 0.05 s. output risk: none by construction. effort 6 to 10 h (ClassifySequence is one long function with a goto; cleanest form is prepare(read) into a heap buffer sized to the read, then resolve(read)).
time-only first test (1 h, no output change): env-switched counter printing the histogram of lookups per read (1-16, 17-64, 65-128, 129-512, >512) and the share of lookups in reads with fewer than 64. if under 10% of lookups are in such reads, drop I1.

### I2. NUMA placement and NUMA balancing
mechanism: pread threads first-touch pages, so the table is spread over both nodes by where those 32 threads ran; about half of lookups cross UPI (1.5 to 2x latency [G]) and, with balancing on, hint faults and THP migrations hit the table during the run. we cannot switch balancing off (no root). without root we can: (a) interleave explicitly with mbind(MPOL_INTERLEAVE) or set_mempolicy before the pread, making placement deterministic and balanced (whether balancing skips such a VMA is a kernel detail I could not verify, so test it); (b) prefault with MADV_POPULATE_WRITE from threads pinned evenly over both sockets (kernel 6.8 has it); (c) numactl --interleave=all as a no-code test, if numactl exists on luna (not checked).
gain at 103 GB [G]: 0 to 10% of the 2 s excess; the load phase (3.2 s) may move a few percent either way because interleaving makes half the zeroing writes remote. output risk: none. effort 2 h.
time-only first test (30 min): cat /proc/vmstat (numa_hint_faults, numa_pages_migrated, thp_migration_success, thp_fault_alloc, thp_fault_fallback, compact_stall) before and after one 103 GB run, and /proc/PID/numa_maps N0=/N1= counts for the table mapping mid run. a delta of numa_hint_faults above about 1M or numa_pages_migrated above about 10k says balancing touches our table (global counters, another job adds noise: repeat twice). then 5 interleaved reps with numactl --interleave=all.

### I3. second-line prefetch for probes that cross a cache line
mechanism: 16 cells per line, load about 0.7, linear probing: a miss lookup scans 6.1 cells and crosses into the next line with probability about 32%, a hit about 7.5% [cycle2 round3_C, Knuth]. the next line is not prefetched, so that access is a fully exposed miss (the L2 adjacent-line prefetcher pairs 128 B halves and may catch some, G). fix: if (idx & 15) >= 12 also prefetch &table_[idx+16] (clamped to capacity). about 25% of lookups issue one extra prefetch; at 1.4% bandwidth use that costs nothing.
gain at 103 GB [A]: 20% of lookups x 250 ns x 218M / 128 = 0.085 s (1.3%). 8/16 GB: under 1%. output risk: none (a prefetch never changes values; keep the address in range anyway). effort 1 h.
time-only first test: the 4-line patch in PrefetchIndex, 5 reps at 103 GB, T=96, K2_B=64. under 1% is inside noise (1 to 3%).

### I4. prefetch hint (t0 / t1 / t2 / nta / w)
GCC maps __builtin_prefetch(p,0,3) = prefetcht0, 2 = t1, 1 = t2, 0 = nta; rw=1 gives prefetchw.
- prefetchw: the table is read-only, nothing to gain, it requests ownership and can add snoops. reject.
- nta: on a non-inclusive L3 like SPR it avoids installing the line in L2/L3. but the hot minimizers are exactly the lines that gain from staying in the 105 MB L3 (81% repeats, LRU-1M hit rate 76%, cycle2). nta would make hot lines miss L3 every time. predicted worse; test only as a sanity row.
- t1/t2: bring the line to L2 (and L3) but not L1. benefit: one-shot random lines stop displacing the scanner's L1 working set, and possibly (not confirmed in any Intel text I could reach) they hold a fill buffer for a shorter time. 2 SMT threads share the 16 LFBs, so 8 each, and a batch of 64 prefetches blocks in the load pipeline until buffers drain. L2-side tracking is larger (a superqueue of 48 is commonly cited; I could not confirm it for Golden Cove). cost: GetAt reads from L2, about 14 cycles (5 ns), instead of L1.
gain at 103 GB [G]: 0 to 3%; 8 GB 0 to 1%. output risk none. effort 0.5 h (4 builds or one macro).
time-only first test: t0 (current), t1, t2, nta at K2_B=64, T=96 and 128, 5 interleaved reps at 103 GB and 8 GB; add perf stat l1d_pend_miss.fb_full and sw_prefetch_access.* (perf worked for the T=96 record).

### I5. TLB and page coverage
arithmetic [A]: 104 GB / 2 MB = 52,000 pages. leaf page-table data is 52k x 8 B = 0.4 MB (the brief said 0.2 MB), which fits L2. the STLB has 2048 entries (reach 4 GB), so about 96% of lookups miss it, and each walk reads a PDE from L2/L3 at 25 to 40 cycles (10 to 15 ns): 218M x 12 ns / 128 = 0.02 s, about 0.3% of the run. with 4 KB pages the PTEs total 104e9 / 4096 x 8 B = 203 MB (the brief's 1.6 GB is a slip: 104 GB / 512 divides by the PTEs per page instead of the page size), larger than L2 and mostly L3, so a walk becomes an L3 or DRAM access: this was the S14 effect (4 KB vs 2 MB, 35 s vs 13-20 s on the old base [M]). so the item is worth something only if some pages are not 2 MB, and vmstat says 99.93% are.
check, no root: cat /proc/PID/smaps_rollup mid run (AnonHugePages about 104 GB); vmstat deltas thp_fault_alloc (expect about 52,000 per run) and thp_fault_fallback; perf stat -e dtlb_load_misses.walk_completed_2m_4m,dtlb_load_misses.walk_completed_4k,dtlb_load_misses.walk_active (all exist on luna, cycle2).
if some pages did fall back: MADV_HUGEPAGE is already set before first touch (correct); populate in parallel with MADV_POPULATE_WRITE so compaction is spread out; khugepaged (10 s sleep, 4096 pages per scan) is too slow to repair 104 GB inside a 7 s run, so repair-after is useless; sysfs knobs need root; 1 GB pages are impossible (hugetlb pool is 0, anonymous 1 GB THP does not exist). start when order-9+ free memory is above 110 GB (as today).
gain [G]: 0 to 1% at 103 GB. effort 0.5 h; it rides on I2's vmstat diff, no extra run.

### I6. radix-cluster a batch by table region
arithmetic [A]: 64 random hashes over 52,000 huge pages give 64^2 / (2 x 52,000) = 0.04 expected same-page pairs per batch (128: 0.16). clustering merges nothing. DRAM row hits are rarer still (8 KB rows over thousands of banks, about 1e-5 per pair). to get one lookup per page you would need about 52k lookups in the batch (about 450 reads), which overflows L1/L2 and delays results. gain 0, cost an extra sort pass (5 to 10 ns per lookup). reject. the only clustering that could matter is by NUMA node (route lookups to the owning socket's threads), which needs cross-thread delegation; not a research-day change.

### I7. AMAC and coroutine interleaving
AMAC (Kocberber, Falsafi, Grot, PVLDB 9(4) 252-263) reports up to 2.3x over state-of-the-art prefetching on irregular lookups and about 4.3x over no prefetching on uniform ones [from a search summary; I did not read the paper body]. its win is irregular chains where group prefetching stalls on the longest chain. ours average 1.07 to 1.32 lines per lookup, nearly uniform, and we already run two-batch group prefetching, the regular case where AMAC only matches it. the coroutine paper (Jonathan et al., VLDB 2018; title confirmed only in a citation list) is a cheaper way to write the same interleaving. so beyond I1 and I3 this is a rewrite worth under 2%. reject. the applicable part of AMAC for us is I1: "processing 2 reads interleaved" is I1.

## 3. what 128 threads x batch 64 means
per thread up to 64 to 128 prefetches are outstanding in software, but only 16 LFBs per core hold L1-missing requests (8 per thread with both SMT threads busy). batch 64 does not mean 64 in flight: the surplus waits in the load pipeline for a free buffer, which is why returns stop between 64 and 128. machine-wide the average stays about 9 lines in flight (section 0), so buffers are not the system limit; the limit is per-thread burstiness (64 prefetches, then 64 resolves) combined with short reads (I1). a rolling prefetch distance of 16 to 32 lookups instead of two bursty batches would smooth the bursts; it only helps if it also crosses reads.

## 4. order of work
zero-code diagnostics first (1.5 h, one 103 GB run plus mid-run cats): vmstat before/after, smaps_rollup AnonHugePages, numa_maps N0/N1 split, perf stat walk events. then the lookups-per-read histogram (I1 test), then the I4 hint builds and the I3 patch (under 1 h of edits each), then I1 and I2 implementation if their tests say so. the cycle2 ladder oracle (fold idx into 4 GB / 128 MB / 8 MB, wrong output, time only) remains the cleanest way to measure the ceiling; the 2 s excess in section 0 already bounds it.

## summary (6 lines)
1. at 103 GB the classification excess over 8 GB is about 2 s of 6.7 s (ceiling for every lookup idea, includes some teardown); at 8/16 GB each idea is worth under 1%.
2. the machine is neither bandwidth bound (1.4% of DRAM peak) nor MLP-capacity bound (about 9 lines in flight vs 1536 buffers); an un-hidden miss costs 2 to 3x nominal latency, so look at NUMA and hint faults, not deeper prefetch.
3. huge pages work: thp_fault_alloc 45.8M vs fallback 31k (99.93%), 130 GB of free memory is in 2 MB+ blocks now; 2 MB walk cost is about 0.3% of the run.
4. numa_balancing=1 (1.15e10 lifetime hint faults, 1.5M THP migrations) is the unmeasured suspect for the 23% kernel time; a vmstat delta around one run settles it with no code.
5. found in the loop: a read with fewer than K2_B lookups gets zero lookahead, average is 116 per read, which explains the 64 to 128 plateau; cross-read pipelining is the real fix.
6. rejected with arithmetic: radix clustering (0.04 page collisions per batch), prefetchw, nta, AMAC beyond I1. sources: AnandTech Golden Cove (16 LFB), PVLDB AMAC record; superqueue 48 and t1/t2 LFB behaviour not confirmed.
