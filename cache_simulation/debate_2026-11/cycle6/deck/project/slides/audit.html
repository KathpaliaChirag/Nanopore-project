Four reasons not to trust the absolute numbers

  • The simulated LLC is one 1.1 MB slice, about 1% of the real 105 MB (and half of the correct size)
  • The no-cache build came from a different source tree: 16 to 34 M extra instructions
  • Cold 10 to 500 read windows, a fixed 45 ns DRAM, no prefetch, TLB or contention model
  • Never calibrated against real performance counters

On the real machine, a front cache covering 45% of lookups gained 0% at 103 GB and was 7% slower at 8 GB.

The real-machine result is the oracle-ceiling test: skip a fixed fraction of the table lookups on Luna, which over-credits any real cache, and measure the wall time (T=32, all 1.87M reads, 3 repetitions). Real-trace facts: 81% of lookups repeat a slot already seen, the 262,144 hottest slots cover 56% of lookups, but the repeats are 130 thousand to 2 million other slots apart, so a small cache forgets them. Net: hardware associativity does not matter for kraken2, and a smarter cache or eviction policy can gain only a few percent once prefetching hides most of the memory latency.
