Fetching ahead does help

How to read it

Height is the time saved against a run with no prefetcher. Each line is a different number of reads.

What we found

Two lines ahead already saves 3% to 8%. Sixteen ahead saves 10.2% at 10 reads and 3.8% at 50 reads.

Why it helps

Parts of kraken2 read memory in order, and a prefetcher can fetch those early. The L1 hit rate rose from 98.86% to 99.45%. This is our reading; we did not measure the parts separately.

The percentage falls as reads are added because part of the early gain is a one-off saving of about 140 microseconds, most likely start-up and input parsing. Between 10 and 50 reads the steady-state saving is about 2.7% on 8 GB and 1.8% on 16 GB. L1 and L2 prefetchers together are best: 11.9%, 5.6% and 4.0% at 10, 25 and 50 reads. The 16 GB database shows the same pattern, 3% to 10%.
