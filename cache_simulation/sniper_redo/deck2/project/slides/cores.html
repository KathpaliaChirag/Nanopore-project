More cores, a bigger prefetching gain

How to read it

One line per read count. Height is the time saved by the L1 and L2 prefetchers together, against a run with no prefetcher. Higher is faster.

What we found

One core: 4% to 12% saved. Four cores: 13% to 16%. Eight and 16 cores: 16% to 19% at every read count. On the 16 GB database at 16 cores: 24.9%.

Caution

At 8 and 16 cores the gain is the same at 10, 25 and 50 reads, yet at 10 reads on 16 cores most of the 64 M instructions are thread start-up. So much of the multi-core gain is likely start-up work. Not proven.

All values are Sniper simulated time, with and without the prefetchers, at the same core count, read count and database. The baseline has no prefetcher at all, so the gain on the real machine, whose CPU already has prefetchers, would be smaller. Instruction counts: 10 reads takes 7.3 M instructions on one core but 32 M on 8 cores and 64 M on 16 cores; 50 reads takes 52 M, 87 M and 130 M. The extra instructions are thread start-up and splitting overhead, and they grow with the core count. That is consistent with the gain growing with cores while staying flat across read counts, but we have not tested it directly. A control (a 4-way LLC change) stayed within 0.9% of base at every core count, so the gain is not noise. Exact values at 8 cores: 18.6, 16.4, 18.2 percent; at 16 cores: 18.2, 15.9, 18.0 percent (10, 25, 50 reads).
