KR2-OPT · THE BOTTLENECKSHEET 02/14

Latency-Bound, Not Compute-Bound

Every measurement points at the same cause

Classifying a read means looking up every minimizer — a representative short DNA fragment — in a hash table built from reference genomes, tens to hundreds of gigabytes, with an essentially random access pattern. The CPU spends most of its time waiting on memory, not calculating.

Reuse rate
Same k-mer looked up again and again — 0.0% reuse measured (M5)
Lever one
Shrink the table — more of it fits in fast memory
Lever two
Catch repeats before they reach the table at all

KR2-OPT · THESIS 1 — MECHANISMSHEET 07/14

The Eviction Policy — Four Fields, One Answer

our design

Sir asked for "biology-dependent adaptive eviction." Nothing more specific than that.

Named sources
Scissorhands (decayed importance) · StreamingLLM (protect universal "sinks") · PIM-Tree (skew-resistant routing) · CachedEmbedding (offline frequency priors)
Genomic translation
"Sinks" = conserved k-mers hit constantly across nearly every sample. Protect them unconditionally; track decayed hit-history for everything else instead of pure recency.
Sizing method
Simulate candidate cache sizes against real read traces, pick the cheapest that hits target hit-rate (Bandana's method) — not a guessed fraction of available cache.
Four literatures that don't cite each other, landing on the same design — that convergence is itself the finding
KR2-OPT · THESIS 1 — EXTENSIONSHEET 08/14

Organism-Blocked, Predictive Partitioning

our design — new this week

A translation of a competing tool's trick, moved down a memory tier

Kun-peng, a competing tool, streams a huge database in 4GB on-demand blocks at the RAM/disk tier to cut memory use. That trick doesn't transfer directly — different scale entirely — but the underlying principle does: don't treat the resident set as one flat pool, partition it into meaningful blocks and decide block-level residency. Here the block is "one organism," at the CPU-cache tier, with a short read-local prediction window on top.
Not yet fully vetted — presented as a real candidate, not a settled result

Reusing the Hash Pair, Deriving the Formula

our design
Bucket placement
Reuse the double-hash pair Thesis 2 already needs to generate 2–4 candidate slots, bucketed 4-way (libcuckoo precedent). Near-free — same two calculations, consumed differently.
False-positive formula for the bitmask cell
